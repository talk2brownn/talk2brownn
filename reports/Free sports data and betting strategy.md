# Free data builds the model; margins decide profit

Free, legitimate data in late 2026 is good enough to build a serious engine for all three sports, but only if you put it together carefully. The backbone is **football-data.co.uk** for football history: free CSVs since 2000 with results, half-time scores, shots, corners, fouls, cards, referees and closing odds from about 20 bookmakers including Pinnacle. Around it sit **API-Football's free tier** (100 calls a day for current stats, lineups, injuries and odds), **Understat** for xG now that FBref has lost its Opta data, the **CC0 Transfermarkt datasets** for goal minutes and lineups (frozen since July 2026), **hoopR's bulk NBA play-by-play files** for quarter scores and leads, the **NBA's own injury PDFs and referee pages**, **API-Handball** plus federation and EHF reports for handball, and **OddsPapi / The Odds API** for live odds, including (by the vendor's claim) Pinnacle and SportyBet. FotMob, FBref, Flashscore, OddsPortal, Sofascore, WhoScored, Squawka, Pinnacle's website and SportyBet itself should be looked at by hand, never scraped. On strategy, the evidence is just as clear and much less comfortable. In top leagues the **closing price is extremely hard to beat** with public data: one 2026 Serie A study gave a good goals model a weight of zero next to the closing odds. The proven skill test is closing line value, not profit. Your accumulator ladder is the biggest problem. At a 7% per-leg margin, typical of SportyBet's 'A or B' markets, a 200-odds rung built from 13 legs returns only about **42 kobo per naira staked** if your picks are no better than the bookmaker's, and **the whole daily ladder loses about 38% of everything staked** before any skill is added. So the engine's job is not to find "safe" bets, because none exist. Its job is to price every market you like from one consistent model, bet only where SportyBet's price beats that model by more than the margin, keep accumulators short, measure itself honestly, and expect SportyBet to limit you if you win.

## Football's free backbone is 25 years of spreadsheets

The single most valuable free football source is still **football-data.co.uk**, run by Joseph Buchdahl. Its free CSVs run from 2000/01 to the current season. Each row holds full-time and half-time results, home and away shots, shots on target, fouls, corners, yellow and red cards, the referee's name (reliably for England), and opening and closing odds from nearly 20 bookmakers, Pinnacle among them ([Substack, "A view from the Pinnacle"](https://networked.substack.com/p/a-view-from-the-pinnacle); [mirror of notes.txt](https://alexandrugris.github.io/assets/data_explanation.txt)). Closing odds are marked with a "C" in the column name, so `PSCH` is Pinnacle's closing price on the home win ([dm13450](https://dm13450.github.io/2019/01/10/Odds-and-Winning.html)). That one detail makes the site priceless: it lets you test any strategy against the sharpest closing price for free. Two caveats matter. First, nobody in this research could confirm that the Pinnacle columns continued after **Pinnacle closed its public API on 23 July 2025** ([Pinnacle API docs](https://github.com/pinnacleapi/pinnacleapi-documentation)), so open a 2025/26 file and check before you build on it. Second, the files have **no xG, no goal minutes, no possession, no throw-ins, no goal kicks, no tackles and no lineups**.

A lesser-known shortcut is **xgabora/Club-Football-Match-Data** on GitHub. It merges football-data.co.uk with ClubElo ratings into **238,858 matches across 27 countries, up to 3 September 2026**, adding Elo ratings, 3- and 5-match form, Bet365 1X2, O/U 2.5 and Asian handicap odds, and max odds across about 17 bookmakers ([GitHub](https://github.com/xgabora/Club-Football-Match-Data-2000-2025)). The repository is MIT-licensed, but the underlying football-data and ClubElo terms still apply.

For goal timing, which you need for "lead by X at any time", "goals in a row" and "both halves" markets, the best free bulk source is the **Transfermarkt datasets** project (CC0). Its tables include 1.27 million game events and 3.17 million lineup rows. However, **its updates stopped in mid-July 2026** and the data ends on 6 July 2026, so it covers no 2026/27 squads ([GitHub](https://github.com/dcaribou/transfermarkt-datasets)). For current-season goal minutes there are three options. **API-Football's free tier** gives 100 requests a day across all endpoints, including fixture events, statistics, lineups, injuries and odds, but only for recent seasons ([API-Football pricing](https://www.api-football.com/pricing); [beginner's guide](https://www.api-football.com/news/post/how-to-get-started-with-api-football-the-complete-beginners-guide)). The little-known **Football Charts API** covers 93 leagues with goal timing and its own model probabilities, at 5,000 calls a day with a key, for personal and research use with attribution ([mcpservers.org listing](https://mcpservers.org/servers/www-football-charts-com-developers)). **OpenLigaDB** gives German leagues with goal minutes and needs no key, though volunteers enter the data, so expect occasional lag or errors ([freeapihub](https://freeapihub.com/apis/openligadb-api)).

xG changed sharply this year. **On 20 January 2026 FBref removed all its Opta advanced data, xG included**, after StatsPerform said the agreement had been breached ([Awful Announcing](https://awfulannouncing.com/soccer/sports-reference-pulls-advanced-data-agreement-violation-dispute.html)). That leaves **Understat** as the only free, ongoing club xG source. It covers the Premier League, La Liga, Bundesliga, Serie A, Ligue 1 and the Russian league from 2014/15. But it has no official API and no published terms, and its endpoints changed in 2026 ([Apify Understat listing](https://apify.com/mirthful_radish/understat-xg-football-scraper); [soccerdata releases](https://github.com/probberechts/soccerdata/releases)). Treat it as "polite, personal research only, expect breakage". For licensed event data, **StatsBomb Open Data** is free with attribution and keeps growing: it added five top women's leagues in May 2026 ([Hudl blog](https://www.hudl.com/blog/statsbomb-free-womens-data-wsl-ligaf-bundesliga-seriea-nwsl); [GitHub](https://github.com/statsbomb/open-data)). The **Wyscout 2017/18 public dataset** (CC BY 4.0) covers every event in the big five leagues for one season ([Figshare](https://figshare.com/collections/Soccer_match_event_dataset/4415000/2)). These two are the only free places to find throw-ins, goal kicks and tackles. They are **for training your own xG and stat models, not for live feeds**.

That gap shapes your favourite match-stat markets. **No free, ongoing, legitimate source of throw-ins or goal kicks for current club seasons turned up anywhere** ([search summary](https://search.r-project.org/CRAN/refmans/mverse/html/soccer.html)). Fouls, corners, cards and referee names are free in football-data.co.uk, so you can build referee card and foul averages yourself by grouping on the Referee column. For upcoming referee appointments, the Premier League publishes official "Match officials for Matchweek N" articles ([Premier League](https://www.premierleague.com/en/news/4688925/match-officials-for-matchweek-1)).

The software layer is equally free. **penaltyblog** (MIT) is the most betting-focused Python library. It includes Poisson, Dixon-Coles, bivariate Poisson and Bayesian models, Elo and pi-ratings, Asian handicap and O/U pricing, and seven methods for removing the bookmaker's margin ([GitHub](https://github.com/martineastwood/penaltyblog); [implied docs](https://penaltyblog.readthedocs.io/en/latest/implied/implied.html)). **soccerdata** is actively maintained with weekly releases ([releases](https://github.com/probberechts/soccerdata/releases)). However, its FBref, WhoScored and SoFIFA readers now rely on CAPTCHA solvers and Cloudflare bypasses, which tells you those sites do not want bots. Use only its football-data.co.uk, ClubElo and Understat readers.

## NBA data is rich and free; handball data must be assembled

Basketball is the best-served sport. The top pick is **hoopR** (R) and its Python twin in sportsdataverse. Its bulk loaders download **pre-built NBA play-by-play for every season from 2002 to 2026** from GitHub releases in about a minute, so you never touch ESPN's servers ([hoopR README](https://stat.ethz.ch/CRAN/web/packages/hoopR/readme/README.html)). From play-by-play you can compute quarter and half scores, team totals, the running margin, the largest lead and "lead by X at any time". These are exactly the inputs for your first-half, quarter, team-total and winning-margin markets. The official stats.nba.com data, reached through **nba_api** and **pbpstats**, adds possessions, lineups on the floor and advanced ratings ([pbpstats docs](https://pbpstats.readthedocs.io/en/latest/pbpstats.data_loader.stats_nba.possessions.html)). The catch is that **stats.nba.com ignores cloud servers** (AWS, Google Cloud, Azure, Colab, GitHub Actions) ([nba_api PyPI](https://pypi.org/project/nba-api/)). The blocking targets data-centre IP ranges, not countries, so a home connection in Nigeria should work even though a cloud server will not. Run nba_api on a home machine and use hoopR files in the cloud. The **wyattowalsh/nbadb** project keeps a ready-built NBA database refreshed on Kaggle as a further fallback ([GitHub](https://github.com/wyattowalsh/nbadb)).

Two official NBA sources are hidden gems. The **NBA injury report** is published as timestamped PDFs several times a day. The latest seen in this research was 9:00 PM on 20 November 2026 ([NBA PDF](https://ak-static.cms.nba.com/referee/injury/Injury-Report_2026-11-20_09_00PM.pdf)), and teams must file game-day reports in fixed windows ([official.nba.com](https://official.nba.com/nba-injury-report-2025-26/)). Because each file is timestamped, an archive of them shows what was known at each hour before tip-off, which is ideal for honest backtesting. **official.nba.com referee assignments** go up at about 9 a.m. ET on game days ([NBA Referee Assignments](https://official.nba.com/referee-assignments)). The page shows only today, so archive it daily if you want referee tendencies. Among the lesser options, **balldontlie's free tier is too thin** at 5 requests a minute and core endpoints only ([SourceForge listing](https://sourceforge.net/software/product/BALLDONTLIE/)). **API-Basketball** gives 100 calls a day for other leagues ([api-sports.io](https://api-sports.io/)). **euroleague-api** wraps EuroLeague and EuroCup play-by-play, box scores and shots ([PyPI](https://pypi.org/project/euroleague-api)).

Handball is the opposite. **No clean, free, bulk handball dataset exists.** The best legitimate starting point is **API-Handball at 100 requests a day**, free indefinitely ([API-Sports handball](https://api-sports.io/sports/handball)). However, whether its free plan covers the Danish 1. Division, especially the women's league, has to be checked with one `/leagues?country=Denmark` call after you sign up. For Danish leagues the master source is the federation's own site, danskhaandbold.dk / hbold.dk ([DHF standings](https://danskhaandbold.dk/puljer/113843/stilling)). Fan sites disagree with each other on dates. For the EHF Champions League, **official EHF match-report PDFs** run to 13 pages, with shot breakdowns, saves, timeouts, punishments and a full event timeline ([EHF match report](https://handballveszprem.hu/_user/file/EHF_OMS_veszprem_kielce.pdf)). Japan's League H publishes on leagueh.jp ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japan_Handball_League)). Wikipedia season pages carry useful league-level totals: the men's Handball-Bundesliga averaged **60.4 goals per match in 2025-26** ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025%E2%80%9326_Handball-Bundesliga)). There is **no free, legitimately licensed handball odds history** ([OddsPapi handball blog](https://oddspapi.io/blog/?p=2901)). The only way to get one is to log odds yourself from today onwards.

## Odds are the scarcest free input and the one that matters most

Odds data decides whether the engine can ever tell value from noise, and it is where free access is thinnest. Pinnacle's public API closed in July 2025 and is now available only to "select high value bettors & commercial partnerships" ([Pinnacle API docs](https://github.com/pinnacleapi/pinnacleapi-documentation)). Every "free Pinnacle" feed since then is a reseller or a scraper. The most generous free option in late 2026 is **OddsPapi**. It offers **250 requests a month**, each returning a whole fixture board, and claims coverage of **Pinnacle and SportyBet** plus handball (EHF, Bundesliga, LNH) ([OddsPapi free API](https://oddspapi.io/blog/?p=144); [SportyBet page](https://oddspapi.io/sportsbooks/sportybet); [handball](https://oddspapi.io/blog/?p=2901)). These are vendor claims. Whether its Pinnacle and SportyBet data is licensed or scraped is unknown, and its own blog posts contradict each other on whether history is free. **The Odds API** gives 500 credits a month. A call costs markets × regions, so it is not 500 requests. Its data runs back to June 2020, historical calls cost 10×, and it has a handball Bundesliga key ([The Odds API handball](https://the-odds-api.com/sports/handball-bundesliga-odds.html); [OddsPapi on its limits](https://oddspapi.io/blog/the-odds-api-free-tier-limits)). Odds from **API-Sports** come on the same 100-a-day key as lineups and injuries. **odds-api.io stopped issuing new free keys on 27 August 2026** ([odds-api.io](https://odds-api.io/blog/free-odds-api)). **Betfair's free delayed key** is for development only. A live key costs £299, commercial use needs approval ([Betfair docs](https://betfair-developer-docs.atlassian.net/wiki/x/URAp)), and one unofficial list puts Nigeria among restricted countries ([vpncentral](https://vpncentral.com/betfair-restricted-countries/)). Using a VPN to get round that breaks Betfair's terms.

For history, football-data.co.uk is the only clean free archive of closing odds. **Betfair's free BASIC historical data** gives only last-traded prices at one-minute intervals with no volume, and practitioners describe it as "of little value" ([betcode FAQ](https://betcode-org.github.io/faq/using-data); [Betfair Data Scientists](https://betfair-datascientists.github.io/modelling/dataSources)). The Kaggle "Beat the Bookie" odds series covers about 114,000 matches in 2015–16. It is useful for learning the method, not for current prices ([GitHub](https://github.com/Lisandro79/BeatTheBookie)). OddsPortal has the deepest handball and basketball odds archive, but **its terms forbid scraping and aggregation** ([OddsPortal terms](https://www.oddsportal.com/terms/)).

SportyBet has **no public odds API**. odds-api.io says outright that it collects SportyBet's "publicly displayed odds" with no affiliation ([odds-api.io SportyBet](https://odds-api.io/sportsbooks/sportybet)), which suggests scraping. In practice you do not need a SportyBet feed. You are the bettor, so the engine only needs its own fair price, and you compare it with the SportyBet price on your screen when you place the bet. The margins you measured from screenshots (4–10%) can be checked against a consensus feed over time.

The free odds budget is tiny: 250 OddsPapi requests plus roughly 125 useful The Odds API calls a month, plus 100 API-Sports calls a day per sport. That forces one design decision. **Take one snapshot early (around 24 hours out) and one near the start (60 to 15 minutes before), cache everything, and store every price you ever see.** After one season you will own a closing-odds archive for NBA, handball and SportyBet itself, which no free source can give you.

## The closing line, not other bettors, is the opponent

Before choosing models, accept what decades of research say about the market you are betting into. In the big five European leagues the 1X2 market is close to efficient. Winkelmann, Ötting and Deutscher studied 14 seasons and found that apparent biases "do not persist systematically over time and across leagues". The number of inefficient league-seasons "barely exceeds what would be expected in an efficient market" ([SSRN 3672233](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=3672233)). The strongest recent result is a 2026 preprint by Pitcan covering **7,220 Serie A matches from 2007 to 2026**. A well-tuned Dixon-Coles goals model was blended with the margin-free closing odds, and the best weight on the model was **0.000**: adding the model only ever made forecasts worse. The market beat the model in all seven test seasons. A shots-on-target model carried information the goals model lacked, but still added nothing beyond the closing price, partly because the close already contains team news ([arXiv 2608.11505](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.11505)). The lesson is not "give up". It is that **a public-data model cannot out-predict the closing line in a top league**. Its value lies in betting before the close, in thinner markets, and in markets the bookmaker prices lazily.

Where has edge actually been documented? Angelini and De Angelis studied 41 bookmakers in 11 leagues and found **4 of 11 markets inefficient when you take the best available odds** ([SSRN 3070329](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=3070329)). Bookmakers were slow to adjust when home advantage vanished in empty stadiums after COVID ([arXiv 2008.05417](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2008.05417)). Kaunitz, Zhong and Kreiner profited by betting whenever a soft bookmaker's odds strayed from the average of all bookmakers, until **bookmakers "began to severely limit our accounts"** ([arXiv 1710.02824](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1710.02824)). Wheatcroft found that football odds **overreact to recent form**, so leaning against hot and cold streaks was profitable in his tests ([JQAS](https://researchonline.lse.ac.uk/id/eprint/115490/1/10.1515_jqas_2019_0009.pdf)). Hubáček and colleagues showed that a model deliberately built to **disagree with the bookmaker** profits more than a merely accurate one ([IJF 2019](http://ida.felk.cvut.cz/zelezny/pubs/ijf.2019.pdf)). A follow-up paper proves why ([Hubáček & Šír 2023](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/intfor/v39y2023i2p691-719.html)). A model that copies the market cannot beat it.

On models, the evidence points to a simple, strong baseline. **Dixon-Coles** is Poisson goals with a correction for low scores and a time-decay that weights recent matches more. It came out best (lowest ranked probability score) in penaltyblog's 2025 comparison against plain Poisson, bivariate Poisson, zero-inflated, negative binomial and Weibull models ([penaltyblog](https://pena.lt/y/2025/03/10/which-model-should-you-use-to-predict-football-matches/)). The right decay speed differs by league: the original paper's 0.0065 per day, against 0.002 in the 2026 Serie A study ([arXiv 2608.11505](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.11505)). So tune it rather than copy it. Inputs beyond goals help. **xG had the highest correlation with future points** in a game-by-game replication ([American Soccer Analysis](https://www.americansocceranalysis.com/home/2022/7/19/the-replication-project-is-xg-the-best-predictor-of-future-results)). A Bundesliga xG model gave "consistent, albeit modest" profit over 11 seasons, with large swings by season ([Wilkens 2026](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/22150218261416681)). **Pi-ratings** beat Elo in Constantinou and Fenton's tests ([pi-ratings paper](http://www.constantinou.info/downloads/papers/pi-ratings.pdf)), though their reported profits were never independently replicated.

**Closing line value (CLV)** is how you find out quickly whether any of this works. CLV means getting a better price than the final, margin-free sharp price. Buchdahl's analysis of about 88,000 Pinnacle football prices found a near one-to-one link between beating the close and long-run yield. A consistent 5% CLV edge shows up as statistically real in about 50 bets, while proving it through profit takes thousands ([Pinnacle Odds Dropper on Buchdahl](https://www.pinnacleoddsdropper.com/blog/closing-line-value--clv-demystified-by-expert-joseph-buchdahl)). Profit and loss is about ten times noisier than CLV: a 2% edge at even money needs roughly 6,800 bets to show up in profit, against about 70 through CLV ([howprosbet summary](https://howprosbet.com/how-to-verify-your-betting-edge/)). Buchdahl still advises 1,000+ bets before drawing serious conclusions ([interview](https://sharpbetting.co.uk/articles/interview-with-joseph-buchdahl)). Two cautions apply. CLV must be measured against a sharp close (Pinnacle or Betfair), not SportyBet's own close. And to remove the bookmaker's margin from odds, the Shin or power method handles the favourite-longshot bias better than simple scaling ([R implied vignette](https://search.r-project.org/CRAN/packages/implied/vignettes/introduction.html)). Both are one function call in penaltyblog.

## One scoreline grid prices every football market you like

Every one of your full-time football markets falls out of one object: a grid of the probability of each exact score, P(home goals, away goals), built by a Dixon-Coles-type model ([penaltyblog docs](https://docs.pena.lt/y/models/dixon_coles.html)). Home win is the sum of all cells where home > away. A team total over 1.5 is the sum of cells where that team has 2 or more. "Home win to nil" adds up the cells where home > away and away = 0. A combo such as "Home & Over 2.5" adds up the cells where both conditions hold. An **"A or B" market such as Home or Over 2.5 is one sum over every cell where either is true**. Never add the two separate probabilities together, because that counts the overlap twice. A worked example with expected goals of 1.6 for the home side and 1.1 for the away side (my calculation) gives Home 48.0%, Over 2.5 50.6%, **Home or Over 2.5 69.0% (fair odds 1.45)** and **Home & Over 2.5 29.6%**. The last figure is well above the 24.3% you would get by naively multiplying the two. If SportyBet prices "Home or Over 2.5" at its typical 7% margin, you would see about 1.35. To have any edge at that price, your model must believe the true chance exceeds **73.9%**, almost five points above this fair value. That is the size of disagreement your favourite markets demand.

Half-based markets need separate first-half and second-half scoring rates, because **about 55–56% of goals come in the second half**. The figures are 55.1% across four top leagues in 2008–11 ([Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11332-013-0154-9)), and roughly 56% since 2005/06 with a range of 54–58% and no long-run trend ([Expecting Goals](https://www.expectinggoals.com/p/the-game-of-two-halves)). Modelling each half as its own Poisson with rates scaled about 45/55 gives a usable first pass for "both halves over/under" and "highest-scoring half" (in the league-average case, my calculation gives both halves over 0.5 at about 53%). Path-dependent markets such as "lead by 2 at any time", "team to score 3 in a row" and "next goal" cannot come from the end-of-match grid at all. They need a **minute-by-minute simulation**. In it, scoring rates rise through the match and peak in the final 15 minutes, and they depend on the score: a leading team slows and a trailing team pushes. This is the Dixon and Robinson birth-process idea, with later work finding the "comeback" effect reverses at a three-goal lead ([arXiv 2611.15378](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.15378); [arXiv 2605.16066](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.16066)). Calibrate the simulation so its full-time results match your scoreline grid and the market's O/U line. **Ignoring game state overstates "lead by 2+" and streak bets on favourites**, because a leading favourite eases off. No published study measures how efficiently bookmakers price these markets, which is why they are worth modelling, but treat any apparent edge as a hypothesis until CLV supports it.

Match-stat props (corners, cards, fouls, shots) are **over-dispersed**: they vary more than a Poisson model allows. The **negative binomial** is the standard fix. In the best academic corner study, a negative binomial with a match-varying shape produced a simulated betting profit of about 5.8% yet **still could not beat the bookmaker on forecast accuracy** ([arXiv 2112.13001](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2112.13001)). A minute-level 2025 study preferred a negative-binomial smooth model for corners and shots ([arXiv 2508.04008](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.04008)). The useful predictors are each team's for-and-against rates, how dominant the match odds say the favourite is, and, for cards and fouls, the referee. Buraimo, Forrest and Simmons showed that **pre-match odds predict cards**: the bigger the home favourite, the fewer yellows it receives and the more the underdog collects. They also noted the cards market became far more efficient after 2001 ([Buraimo et al.](https://college.holycross.edu/hcs/RePEc/spe/BuraimoForrestSimmons_Refereeing.pdf)). Vendor blogs claim EPL referees ranged from 2.74 to 4.68 yellows a game in 2025/26 and that soft books price at the league average ([statpair](https://statpair.com/blog/betting-cards-yellow-red-statistical-analysis)). That is plausible but unverified, so test it on your own football-data referee table. One practitioner pipeline blends its corner model **50/50 with the line the market implies** ([GitHub](https://github.com/Git4AlexD/football-corners-predictor)). That kind of humility keeps a props model from chasing its own errors. With your observed **6% corners margin** and **9% throw-in/goal-kick margin**, and no free current data at all for throw-ins and goal kicks, the honest conclusion is that **throw-in and goal-kick props should stay out of the engine**.

## NBA periods split cleanly; handball goals behave like coin-flips

NBA pricing rests on two well-supported numbers. First, the final margin is roughly normal around the closing spread with a **standard deviation of about 11.6–12 points** ([Gelman on Stern](https://statmodeling.stat.columbia.edu/2016/10/28/warriors-suck-bayesian-exploration/); [Wayne Winston](https://waynewinston.com/wordpress/p_2333/)). Second, expected points are pace × efficiency. For your period markets, hobbyist analysis of 2,427 recent games found the **first-half total averages 50.2% of the full-game line (SD 11.9) and the first quarter 25.3% (SD 8.1)**. The same analysis found **no team-specific quarter or half "share" edge** that survived testing against the market ([MJACode PR #1206](https://github.com/MJACode/betting-model/pull/1206)). That is hobbyist work, not peer-reviewed. In plain terms, near the line each point you are right by is worth about 3.3% win probability on a spread. On your **6%-margin quarter totals** the break-even is 53%, which means the true quarter mean must sit about 0.6 points from the line *and* your model must know it. On **5%-margin team totals** the break-even is 52.5%.

The efficiency research is old but consistent. NBA spreads and totals were found efficient over 2000–2008 ([The Sport Journal](https://thesportjournal.org/article/nba-gambling-inefficiencies-a-second-look/)). Overs on high totals were over-bet, but not by enough to beat the margin ([Paul, Weinbach & Wilson](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/quaeco/v44y2004i4p624-632.html)). The one repeated anomaly is **early-season totals: 58.2% of first-week games went under in 2009–12**, after which the market was "generally very efficient" ([J. Prediction Markets](https://ircommons.uwf.edu/esploro/outputs/journalArticle/Early-season-NBA-overunder-bias/99380090347306600)). That data is more than a decade old, so re-test it each October. Rest matters: **3+ rest days against a back-to-back is worth about 1.3–2.3 points of margin** ([Entine & Small](https://faculty.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2012/04/Nba.pdf); [NBAAnalytic](https://nbaanalytic.com/articles/the-rest-ledger.html)). The market prices this in, so edge comes only from late injury or rest news, which is exactly what the timestamped injury PDFs capture. Comebacks are more common than old models say: a team down 20 now wins about 5.3% of the time, against 2.9% before 2017 ([nba-comeback-dashboard](https://nba-comeback-dashboard.github.io/analysis/20_is_new_18.html)). Any "lead by X" or winning-margin pricing therefore needs play-by-play simulation built on recent seasons. Claims about referee crews shifting totals by 1–3 points come only from a blog with no sample size ([nbabettingsystem](https://nbabettingsystem.com/articles/nba-referee-tendencies-and-betting/)).

Handball behaves differently from football. A team's goals fit a **binomial distribution** better than a Poisson (lots of shots, each with a fairly fixed chance of scoring), and **home advantage is about 2 goals**, with a 58.9% home win rate in the 2010/11 Bundesliga ([Groll et al., arXiv 1207.0700](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1207.0700)). The EHF Champions League averaged **57.6 goals with about 9% draws** in 2014–24 (blog figure) ([oddstricks](https://oddstricks.co.uk/articles/european-handball-goal-margin-distributions-identifying-arb-windows-in-quarter-margins-across-major-leagues/)). In practice, model each team's goals as roughly normal around a rating-based expectation, with a margin SD near 6 goals, and check draw rates against the market (my normal approximation with a 2-goal home edge gives about 6% draws, a little low). **Ratings (Elo, PageRank, pi-ratings) are the strongest handball predictors**, getting women's scores to within about 3.8 goals ([Felice & Ley 2025](https://doi.org/10.1177/22150218251313937)). Treat women's leagues as separate populations: Bundesliga women's totals ran from 56.4 to 61.0 between seasons ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025%E2%80%9326_Handball-Bundesliga_(women))). **No study of handball betting-market efficiency exists.** Thin markets like the Danish 1. Division are plausibly softer, but SportyBet's 8–10% handball margins put the break-even on a two-way line near 54–55%, which demands a real edge.

## A 200-odds ladder returns about 42 kobo per naira

Accumulator maths is simple and unforgiving. If each leg returns, on average, *r* per unit staked (r = 1 divided by 1 plus the margin when your picks are only as good as the bookmaker's), then an accumulator returns **r multiplied by itself once per leg**. Bloomberg's analysis of US sportsbook data found that parlay bettors **lose 19 cents per dollar on average** ([Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/features/2026-sports-betting-debt-bankruptcy/)). Same-game parlay margins rose from 13.1% to 18.5% between 2019 and 2023 ([iGamingNews](https://www.igamingnews.biz/same-game-parlay-sportsbook-margin-engine/)). Here is your ladder at SportyBet's observed margins, assuming no edge (my calculations). The first set of columns builds each rung from legs priced around 1.50 at a 7% margin ("A or B"-type legs); the second uses legs around 1.90 at a 5% margin (totals-type legs).

| Target odds | Legs at ~1.50 | Expected return per ₦1 (7% legs) | Chance of winning | Legs at ~1.90 | Expected return per ₦1 (5% legs) | Chance of winning |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| ~2 | 2 | ₦0.87 | about 1 in 3 | 1 | ₦0.95 | about 1 in 2 |
| ~5 | 4 | ₦0.76 | 1 in 7 | 3 | ₦0.86 | 1 in 8 |
| ~10 | 6 | ₦0.67 | 1 in 17 | 4 | ₦0.82 | 1 in 16 |
| ~20 | 7 | ₦0.62 | 1 in 27 | 5 | ₦0.78 | 1 in 32 |
| ~50 | 10 | ₦0.51 | 1 in 113 | 6 | ₦0.75 | 1 in 63 |
| ~100 | 11 | ₦0.48 | 1 in 182 | 7 | ₦0.71 | 1 in 126 |
| ~200 | 13 | ₦0.42 | 1 in 469 | 8 | ₦0.68 | 1 in 251 |

Two lessons jump out. First, **staking equally on all seven rungs of the 1.50-leg ladder loses about 38% of everything staked**, on average, before skill. By coincidence, that matches the 38.3% that flat-stake followers of three prominent Nigerian tipsters lost across 5,467 tracked slips ([arXiv 2604.08251](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.08251)). Second, **fewer, longer-priced legs hurt far less than many short ones**, because margin is charged per leg. If you must reach 200, eight legs at 1.90 beat thirteen at 1.50. "Safe" short legs are an illusion: five 1.20 favourites that each win 80% of the time give odds of about 2.5 but win only a third of the time and lose about 18.5% on average. Favourites do carry less margin than longshots, thanks to the favourite-longshot bias ([Whelan](https://www.karlwhelan.com/Papers/Monopoly.pdf)), so short legs are less bad, but still negative unless each leg has its own edge. Accumulator bonuses do not rescue this. A 10% bonus on a 6-leg acca at 5% margins still leaves an expected loss of about 19% (my calculation).

There is one genuine structural edge, and it comes from **correlation**. In the worked example, Home & Over 2.5 has a true joint chance of 29.6%. If a bookmaker let you put "Home" and "Over 2.5" from the same match into a normal multi and simply multiplied their 5%-margin prices (about 1.98 × 1.88 = 3.73), that slip would be worth **about +10% in expected value** (my calculation). This is exactly why bet-builders charge a correlation premium, measured at 3.7–6.8% on one test slip ([sharpfootballanalysis](https://www.sharpfootballanalysis.com/sportsbook/same-game-parlay-sites/)), and why books usually block related legs. Whether SportyBet's ordinary multi allows same-match combinations, and how it prices its own combo and "A or B" markets compared with your grid, is unknown. Test it by hand. Across different matches, legs are essentially independent, so correlation neither helps nor hurts.

Staking cannot create an edge; it only manages one. The **Kelly stake** is (p × odds − 1) / (odds − 1). For example, a 55% chance at 1.91 gives 5.5% of bankroll. Full Kelly is too aggressive whenever your probabilities are uncertain, and betting double Kelly or more gives zero or negative growth ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kelly_criterion); [Hubáček & Šír review](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2107.08827)). **Half-Kelly keeps about 75% of the growth with half the swings**, and quarter-Kelly or flat 1–2% stakes are safer still while edges are unproven. Thorp advised cutting stakes on simultaneous bets that are correlated ([Thorp 2007](https://web.williams.edu/Mathematics/sjmiller/public_html/341/handouts/Thorpe_KellyCriterion2007.pdf)). Martingale and loss-chasing change nothing about expected value and only add ruin risk. Accumulators also wreck your ability to learn. Using the standard sample-size rule (my calculation), confirming a 5% edge takes about 1,600 bets at odds of 2, about 14,400 at odds of 10, and **over 300,000 at odds of 200**. You will never know from results alone whether your top rungs work.

Finally, plan for success being punished. **Only about 4% of 700,000+ US online gamblers withdrew more than they deposited** over 2019–23 ([UCSD](https://today.ucsd.edu/story/legalized-gambling-increases-irresponsible-betting-behavior-especially-among-low-income-populations)). High-volume bettors expect to break even but lose 7.5 cents per dollar ([Brown](https://mattbrownecon.github.io/assets/papers/jmp/sportsbetting.pdf)). The Kaunitz team, who did win, were restricted within months ([MIT Technology Review](https://www.technologyreview.com/2017/10/19/67760/the-secret-betting-strategy-that-beats-online-bookmakers/)). No public information exists on SportyBet's limiting policy, but soft books in general limit winners.

## Conclusion: what this means for our build

The engine is best understood as a **pricing and discipline machine**: it says what each SportyBet market is truly worth and refuses most bets. It is not a tip generator. The table maps each component to its data and methods.

| Engine component | Free data feeding it | Method from the evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Historical store | football-data.co.uk + xgabora (football), Transfermarkt CC0 (goal minutes to July 2026), hoopR bulk files (NBA), API-Handball + DHF/EHF (handball) | Load once into DuckDB/SQLite; refresh weekly |
| Daily fixtures and team news | API-Football/Basketball/Handball (100/day each), NBA injury PDFs, official.nba.com referees, Premier League officials | Pull injuries the morning of; lineups 60–20 min before kick-off; archive every PDF and referee page |
| Odds logger (most important) | OddsPapi (Pinnacle + SportyBet claims), The Odds API, API-Sports odds, plus your own SportyBet screen prices | Two snapshots per fixture (T-24h, T-1h); store everything; builds your own closing archive |
| Ratings layer | ClubElo, own pi-ratings/Elo (football, handball), pace and efficiency (NBA) | Pi-ratings and Elo as features; ratings are the best handball predictor |
| Football scoreline engine | Goals + Understat xG + shots on target | Dixon-Coles with tuned decay (penaltyblog); prices 1X2, DC, O/U, team totals, win to nil, combos and "A or B" as unions over one grid |
| Path simulator | Transfermarkt/API-Football goal minutes; hoopR play-by-play | Minute-level (football) and possession-level (NBA) Monte Carlo with score-dependent rates; calibrate to the grid and the market line |
| Props engine | football-data corners, fouls, cards, referee | Negative binomial with odds-implied dominance and a referee effect; blend 50/50 toward the market line; **skip throw-ins and goal kicks** |
| NBA engine | hoopR, nba_api (from home), injury PDFs | Normal model: margin SD about 12, 1H ≈ 50% and Q1 ≈ 25% of the total; early-season unders as a test, not a rule |
| Handball engine | API-Handball, DHF, EHF PDFs | Normal/binomial goals, about 2-goal home edge, separate women's models, check draw calibration |
| Market blend | Sharp prices from OddsPapi/football-data; multi-book average elsewhere | Remove margin with Shin/power; fit the market weight out of sample (expect it to be high in top leagues) |
| Value filter | SportyBet price at bet time | Bet only if model probability × SportyBet odds clears 1 plus a buffer that is larger for higher-margin markets |
| Ladder builder | Value-filtered legs only | Fewest legs possible; prefer longer-priced value legs; leave a rung empty when value is lacking; test same-match combos by hand |
| Staking | Bankroll ledger | Quarter-Kelly or flat 1–2% on singles; a small fixed "lottery" budget for rungs above 20 |
| Evaluation | Logged closes | CLV against the sharp close, log loss and Brier against the de-margined market, reliability plots; review after every 250 bets and judge after 1,000+ |

Three conclusions follow from putting the two halves of this research together. First, **the odds logger is worth more than any model**. Free sharp history ends with football, so a season of your own snapshots, SportyBet included, is the asset that makes everything else testable. Second, **your favourite markets are exactly the ones a scoreline grid and a path simulator handle better than intuition**: "A or B", combos, win to nil, both halves, lead-by-X and quarter lines. They are also where SportyBet's margins are highest, so the engine should expect to pass on most of them and act only on large disagreements. Its real contribution will be stopping bad bets, not producing good ones. Third, the ladder should flip its logic. Instead of choosing a target price and filling it with legs, the engine should list today's value legs and build only the rungs those legs can honestly support. No bet is safe, margin compounds with every leg, and if CLV ever proves an edge, SportyBet will likely limit the account. Building that expectation into the plan from day one is what separates an engine from a lottery ticket.
